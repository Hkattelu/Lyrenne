# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Lyrenne serves Windows desktop listeners who want YouTube Music without keeping a browser in their workspace. They may use a traditional desktop, a compact tiled window manager, keyboard shortcuts, media keys, or the Windows system media controls. Their primary job is to find, start, and manage music while staying focused on other work.

## Product Purpose

Lyrenne provides a focused, native-feeling YouTube Music client for Windows. Success means playback, discovery, library management, and queue control remain dependable and easy to reach without the weight or visual noise of a general-purpose browser.

## Brand Personality

Calm, deliberate, familiar. Lyrenne should feel purpose-built and quietly distinctive while preserving the affordances people already understand from mature desktop media players.

## Anti-references

Lyrenne should not resemble a stock Material component showcase, a website merely wrapped in a desktop window, or a decorative gamer terminal. Avoid card-heavy layouts, unexplained chrome, ornamental status readouts, gratuitous animation, and styling that competes with album artwork.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the music primary. Artwork, track identity, playback state, and the next useful action should dominate the hierarchy.
2. Feel native to a keyboard-driven Windows workspace. Navigation and controls should remain compact, predictable, and efficient at the minimum supported window size.
3. Earn familiarity before novelty. Standard player and navigation affordances should work as expected, with character coming from proportion, typography, and restrained accents.
4. Use one coherent visual vocabulary. Surfaces, borders, controls, focus states, and spacing should follow shared tokens across every screen.
5. Make customization a layer, not a fork in behavior. Personal styling should not weaken accessibility, platform integration, or maintainability.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG AA contrast for readable text and controls. Preserve complete keyboard navigation and visible focus states, do not communicate state through color alone, and keep touch targets and spacing usable without making the desktop interface feel oversized. Motion should convey state, stay brief, and respect reduced-motion expectations.
